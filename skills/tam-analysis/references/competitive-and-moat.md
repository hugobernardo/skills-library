# Competitive analysis and moat assessment

Define competition broadly. The most dangerous competitor is usually the one that defines itself differently — a platform that bundles the capability for free, or the status quo.

## Competitor taxonomy (cover all six)

| Class | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Direct** | Same core customer + same core problem + similar solution |
| **Adjacent** | Solves part of the same workflow |
| **Vertical specialist** | Solves the problem in one category |
| **Platform-native** | Capability built by the systems of record, marketplaces or platforms the customer already uses — often free or bundled |
| **Internal build** | Large customers or platforms solving it themselves |
| **Status quo** | Manual work, spreadsheets, agencies, custom integrations, doing nothing — the actual incumbent for most of the universe |

## Competitor table

One row per dimension, one column per meaningful competitor plus the company. Dimensions to adapt: target customer · core product · integration coverage · key capabilities (name the 4–6 that matter in this market) · pricing · geographic strength · distribution model · footprint · funding/resources · partnerships · data advantage · key advantage · key weakness.

Rules: do not manufacture comparisons — write **Unknown** where data is unavailable. Separate **observed** capability (deployed, evidenced) from **announced** (press release, roadmap) from **inferred** (analyst judgment). A marketing announcement is never proof of deployment.

## Competitive red team (per major competitor)

- **Why the company could win** — structural advantages only (integration coverage, legacy compatibility, distribution partnerships, accumulated data, switching costs, network, operating experience), not marketing claims.
- **Why the competitor could win** — with equal rigor: scale, brand, distribution ownership, capital, existing relationships, modern architecture, UX, pricing, network effects, proprietary data, native integration, ability to bundle the functionality for free.
- **Build-vs-partner risk** for every major platform: *Why would this platform partner instead of building it?* and *What could cause it to internalize the company's role?* Treat as a material strategic risk, not a footnote.

If a competitor is stronger in a strategically important dimension, say so plainly.

## Moat assessment

Grade each candidate advantage **Strong / Moderate / Weak / Not Yet Proven**, separately for **current moat** and **potential future moat**:

proprietary technology · accumulated integrations · proprietary data · distribution · network effects · density · brand/partner relationships · channel relationships · switching costs · operational know-how · cost advantages · regulatory advantages · data rights.

For each, answer: *Could a well-funded competitor reproduce this, and with roughly what combination of time, capital, relationships and data?* Something is not defensible because it took work to build. Accumulated integrations are frequently engineering effort, not a moat, unless they cover segments no one else will economically serve.

## Competitive map

Position competitors on the two axes that are strategically meaningful in this market (e.g., "customer segment: modern stack ↔ legacy stack" × "scope: single channel ↔ many channels"). Avoid generic axes like "feature completeness."

## Output into the Source of Truth

§Competitive Truth: direct competitors · important substitutes · platform threats · where the company is stronger · where weaker · what must become true for a durable advantage. §Right-to-Win: proven today · emerging · aspirational (evidence-supported only). Profiles with dated observed/announced/inferred entries go in `G_Competitor_Profiles.md` with `COMP-` IDs.
