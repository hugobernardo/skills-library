# Red team, scoring, verdicts, and quality control

## Skeptical analyst review (run after the first full draft, in every mode)

Switch roles: you are an investment analyst whose compensation depends on finding flaws. Challenge every major conclusion on:

1. **Market inflation** — adjacent markets the company cannot actually monetize.
2. **Denominator inflation** — counts that include businesses that would never buy.
3. **ARPU inflation** — pricing unsupported by demonstrated willingness to pay.
4. **Adoption assumptions** — assuming adoption because the product creates value.
5. **Channel dependency** — excessive reliance on one platform, partner or distributor.
6. **Platform risk** — could an ecosystem participant remove the company from the value chain?
7. **Vertical fragmentation** — does each segment need different catalog, integration, support, sales?
8. **Integration moat** — defensible, or accumulated engineering work?
9. **Data moat** — legal, contractual and commercial right to aggregate and monetize?
10. **Competitive blind spots** — stronger substitutes ignored because they define themselves differently.
11. **Unit economics** — can the TAM support attractive CAC, onboarding, support and gross margin?
12. **TAM ≠ opportunity** — large TAM, poor venture-scale opportunity?
13. **Multiple TAMs** — are "different markets" the same customer monetized several ways?
14. **Distribution** — is there a scalable route to the whole serviceable market, or a handful of doors?

Revise conclusions where the critique is compelling. Record what changed (and what was re-derived and retained) as DEC entries. Typical outputs: an assumption promoted to Critical; a dependency elevated; a sensitivity no draft had flagged.

## Investment-committee objections

Write the strongest evidence-based version of at least five objections — always including: *"The market only sounds large because several adjacent industries are added together; this is actually a small integration/tooling vendor."* For each: the argument, then whether the evidence **refutes / partially refutes / supports** it.

## Evidence challenge (per core thesis)

For each `THESIS-` entry: evidence supporting · evidence contradicting · what remains unknown · what evidence would falsify it. Verdicts: **Strongly Supported / Supported / Plausible but Unproven / Weak / Contradicted**. Do not omit contradictory evidence; do not let a thesis persist because it became part of the company narrative.

## Confidence scoring (per market, 1–5)

Market-size confidence · source quality · customer pain · willingness to pay · competitive attractiveness · right-to-win · distribution advantage · product readiness · defensibility · strategic importance. Explain every score below 4.

## Market attractiveness ranking

Rank markets on: TAM · realistic SAM · achievable 5-year SOM · growth · urgency of customer problem · willingness to pay · competitive intensity · right-to-win · GTM difficulty · product-development burden · channel dependency · strategic synergy with the existing network. TAM alone never determines the ranking.

Market verdicts: **Highly Attractive / Attractive / Promising but Unproven / Strategic Option / Unattractive**.

## Final verdict

1. Is there a venture-scale market? **Yes / Probably / Possibly / Probably Not / No** — explained.
2. Most important market today.
3. Greatest long-term upside.
4. What to avoid or deprioritize.
5. What must be proven in 12–24 months: 5–10 measurable milestones that would materially raise confidence. Tie each to the OQ it resolves.

## Analytical rules that most often get broken

- Prefer a smaller number with strong evidence over a larger one with weak evidence.
- Bottom-up must never be reverse-engineered to match top-down.
- Avoid arbitrary "% of market" where observable operating units exist.
- Separate today's product from plausible future products; label optionality as optionality.
- Distinguish market growth from the company's ability to monetize it; do not trust a research-firm forecast because of who published it.
- Treat incumbent distribution and platform control as competitive advantages against the company.
- Where evidence does not support a claim, write **"Not sufficiently supported"** rather than filling the gap with inference.
- The recommendation reflects the strength of the evidence, not the attractiveness of the narrative.

## Final quality-control gate (all twelve must be yes)

1. Can every important TAM input be traced to evidence?
2. Were conflicting market definitions reconciled?
3. Were TAM calculations independently (re)built?
4. Were weak sources prevented from driving headline conclusions?
5. Are estimates clearly distinguished from facts?
6. Was double counting removed?
7. Are competitor advantages documented as well as the company's?
8. Did the red team materially challenge the conclusions?
9. Are unresolved questions visible?
10. Could a new team member understand both the conclusion and why it was reached?
11. Could an investor challenge a headline and immediately see the calculation and sources?
12. Can future research update individual assumptions without rebuilding the analysis?
