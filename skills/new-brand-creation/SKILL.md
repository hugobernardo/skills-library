# New Brand Creation

Create a brand that expresses what the product or business actually does, then make the result usable by the people building it.

## Choose the mode

- **Full creation:** positioning through name, identity, system, and handoff.
- **Focused exploration:** only the unresolved layer, such as naming or logo direction. Preserve decisions already made.
- **Handoff:** package a selected identity for implementation without reopening it.

Read [references/exploration-and-convergence.md](references/exploration-and-convergence.md) for full creation or any work that still needs strategic/creative convergence. Read [references/handoff-package.md](references/handoff-package.md) when producing files for designers, developers, or other agents.

## Core principles

### Product truth before brand theater

Anchor the identity in the product's role, behavior, desired feeling, audience, and non-negotiables. A brand may endorse good product decisions; it must not overrule usability or create cosmetic changes merely to prove the brand exists.

Distinguish durable philosophy from changeable expression. Product UI and visual trends can evolve. The brand's behavioral promise should be more resilient.

### Explore before naming

Map genuinely different conceptual territories before generating names or marks. Prevent the user's starting metaphor, competitors, or the first attractive idea from becoming an invisible brief.

Keep the process interactive. Present compact, legible sets; collect pull, rejection, indifference, and reasoning; then use that feedback to reshape the field. Do not bury the user in an undifferentiated name dump.

### Let each product remain itself

For umbrella or portfolio brands, decide explicitly whether the relationship is monolithic, endorsed, or independent. A quiet signature is often more useful than forced visual family resemblance. Never import umbrella colors, typography, or components into products solely to make the portfolio look related.

### Make the visual work tangible

When the user asks for a logo or identity, create actual visual studies and inspect the renders. Prefer editable SVG for geometric marks, lockups, and identity boards. Use image generation only when raster illustration, texture, or imagery is genuinely part of the concept.

Unintended readings are design data. A mark resembling a leaf, animal, arrow, or office template may be acceptable, useful, or disqualifying depending on what it implies. Discuss the implication rather than pretending the reading is not there.

### Verify current viability

Before recommending a final name, browse for current exact and near matches in relevant markets, app stores, domains, and obvious social handles. Evaluate confusion risk, not merely whether an exact `.com` is available. Treat domain status as transient and never claim trademark clearance; state when formal legal review remains necessary.

## Working sequence

Adapt the sequence to what is already decided:

1. Frame the product/business truth, desired relationship, behavioral promise, boundaries, and brand architecture.
2. Explore distinct territories and converge through user feedback.
3. Develop and screen names; recommend one with clear reasoning and named risks.
4. Lock the verbal core: name, descriptor, brand line, product promise, voice, and a boundary statement describing what the brand will not become.
5. Explore visual directions; compare actual renders; refine the strongest mark and wordmark.
6. Build the system: palette, typography roles, layout principles, imagery/iconography, motion, accessibility, and product-expression rules.
7. Package approved assets and guidance for implementation. Keep explorations visibly separate from production assets.

Do not force every step when the user already has a strong answer. Ask focused questions only when the missing choice would materially change the result.

## Completion standard

A complete brand should leave the user with:

- A recommended, screened name and its rationale.
- A concise verbal identity and behavioral boundary.
- An approved mark, wordmark/lockup direction, and relevant icon variants.
- A coherent visual system with accessible, semantic tokens.
- Clear rules for product expression and misuse.
- An implementation-ready handoff when other people or agents will apply the brand.

Do not call a concept final while critical items remain provisional. Label live-font wordmarks, unresolved rights, untested small-size marks, inferred dark themes, and pending name clearance honestly.
